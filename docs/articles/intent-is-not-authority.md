# Intent is not authority: what an agent gateway cannot promise, and what it can

If your product has an agent that can issue a refund, release a payout, write a booking, trigger a deploy or change an entitlement, you will be offered a gateway that promises to make those calls safe. Agent Middleware API (AMW) is one. This article states what the gateway can and cannot promise, refutes three claims you may hear about it, reports what the repository's own tests support, and lists what you still have to build. Three evidence classes are kept apart and labelled: industry evidence, AMW's measured results with the file that proves each, and hypotheses not yet measured.

## Thesis

AMW provides an operator-controlled boundary for consequential tool calls. Scoped permits constrain gateway-authorized actions; idempotency controls deduplicate tested same-key requests; signed receipts provide verifiable records of gateway decisions and observed outcomes. These controls do not guarantee exactly-once downstream effects. Their effectiveness depends on deployment configuration, protected signing keys, closed bypass routes, durable state and upstream reconciliation.

The organizing idea is that intent is not authority. An agent can decide to refund a customer; that decision is not permission. Permission is a signed permit bound to a wallet, a tool scope, a budget and an expiry. Before reserving budget or dispatching, the gateway validates wallet binding, key binding, scope, budget, expiry, revocation and signature (TRUST_MODEL.md:6-30). It does not validate argument shape; only the tool does (docs/failure-lab-suite.md:290-295).

Who may mint a permit is a wallet-authority question, not an operator-only one. A key with access to the issuer wallet can mint a permit for that wallet or a wallet it funds, and that includes the agent's own wallet-scoped key (app/routers/permits.py:101-120; docs/quickstart.md:114-120). On the standard `POST /mcp` surface the server materializes a permit from wallet policy at call time (docs/tool-interface-authority.md:8-11). What bounds the agent is custody of the wallet key and the wallet's funding and policy. For authority beyond what its key can self-issue, the agent can ask through `POST /v1/permit-requests`; a human decides in Sentinel, an external approval service the operator must configure, and the permit is minted from the reviewed terms (README.md:240-241; WEDGE.md:174-177; app/services/permit_requests.py:1-37).

## Three claims that do not hold

**"Exactly-once downstream."** No. The gateway guarantees at most one dispatch and at most one wallet debit per accepted idempotency key. The dispatch claim is committed before the network send, so it records the authority to send, not that a send happened, and never that the tool acted. The remote effect is once only if the upstream tool also honours the forwarded key (README.md:157-162; WEDGE.md:16-23).

**"Any retry is safe from duplicating a refund or payout."** No. Same-key retries are deduplicated, and that is tested. A fresh-key retry with identical arguments is dispatched again and receipted as a success under the shipped default, which is log mode (tests/test_upstream_retry_cap_enforcement.py:527-528; app/core/config.py:148), and the failure lab measures it as two dispatches and two debits (tests/test_failure_lab.py:188-192). The single-run lab's agent-restart case pays twice in both configurations it runs that case under, native and gateway; behind the gateway that is two dispatches, two debits and two receipts (tests/test_failure_lab.py:183-199; docs/failure-lab.md:110-111, 128-133). A signed receipt records what the gateway observed; it does not independently prove downstream payment completion.

**"0% attack success."** Not a claim this repository can make. Its permit-validation battery refuses ten reason codes with no debit to either wallet on a local quickstart posture; eight return a signed denial receipt, and `permit_required` and `permit_not_found` fail closed without one (scripts/red_team_trust_plane.py; tests/test_red_team_trust_plane.py:39-53; docs/PROOF_MATRIX.md:72-79). That is a validation battery, not an attack-success rate. The Open Agent Passport paper, sometimes cited for a zero result, reports it under a restrictive policy that allowed zero capabilities and a $0 limit (Table 6). That demonstrates blocking, not equally useful operation with perfect security.

## What the industry evidence shows

*Evidence class: industry. Surveys, disclosed vulnerabilities and regulation. None of it measures AMW.*

The Cloud Security Alliance press release of 21 April 2026 reports that 65% of respondents' organizations had experienced AI agent-related incidents in the past 12 months. That figure comes from an online survey of 418 IT and security professionals, run in January 2026 and commissioned by Token Security. It is a survey result, not a measured rate across all organizations. IBM's report of 30 July 2025 says 13% of the breached organizations it studied reported a breach of AI models or applications, and 97% of that subset reported lacking proper AI access controls; that statistic concerns AI models and applications, not agents specifically.

The incidents and disclosures commonly cited in this debate do not establish accidental duplicate execution. EchoLeak (CVE-2025-32711), a zero-click flaw in Microsoft 365 Copilot found by Aim Labs and disclosed in June 2025 after Microsoft patched it, was a disclosed vulnerability with no known affected customers, not a confirmed theft incident. Step Finance blamed the January 2026 theft from its treasury, which it put at about $40 million, on compromised executive devices; nobody has established that autonomous agents executed those transfers, so it is not used here as agent evidence. Both belong to the class of compromised credentials, malicious instructions, supply-chain exposure and exfiltration, and neither says anything about retries.

Those classes map poorly onto what AMW does. By the repository's own OWASP self-assessment, credential misuse is enforced only for DB-backed wallet-scoped keys (bootstrap-admin keys are full-control credentials the claim does not cover); prompt injection is contained rather than prevented, because the gateway cannot see prompts; supply chain is partially addressed because there is no registry to poison and the single upstream origin is validated and pinned. The mapping has no row for data exfiltration, and no document in the repository claims to address it (docs/owasp-agentic-top10-mapping.md).

AMW's idempotency argument therefore has to stand on its own evidence. The repository's own duplicate-execution evidence is three GitHub issue reports and one community thread: stripe/ai#402 and crewAIInc/crewAI#5802 are reproductions, langchain-ai/langgraph#7417 is a confirmed production incident of duplicated execution and cost, and the community thread is a first-hand report of one request firing a side effect four times. The repository records no confirmed production incident of a duplicated payment charge (docs/market-research-2026-08.md:57-73, 326-328).

On regulation, the EU AI Omnibus, a regulation in force since 27 July 2026, moved the application date for stand-alone high-risk AI systems listed in Annex III to 2 December 2027, and for high-risk AI embedded in products covered by Annex I product legislation to 2 August 2028. The EUR 35m / 7% of turnover penalty tier concerns prohibited practices, not ordinary high-risk compliance failures generally. AMW claims no compliance mapping to the EU AI Act or any other framework; receipts may be one input an auditor accepts, and that determination is the operator's (WEDGE.md:292-295). AEGIS, a pre-execution firewall and audit layer for agent tool calls, is a research system described in a 2026 preprint, not an established standard.

## What AMW's measured results show

*Evidence class: measured. In-repo tests against fake executors (in-process, plus an opt-in two-process SIGKILL proof against PostgreSQL), two failure labs on throwaway SQLite, invariant attacks on the local quickstart posture with a PostgreSQL control, and probes of the operator's own production deployment that touched only its unauthenticated layer and health endpoints. None measures the governed permit, invoke and receipt path under production traffic, and none involves a customer or external caller.*

**Same key, same payload.** One dispatch, one debit, the same receipt id; on a local tool the tool body ran once. Proof: tests/test_adversarial_five_claims.py:345-353; tests/test_upstream_retry_cap_enforcement.py:656-657.

**Same key, changed payload.** Refused with `idempotency_key_reused`, no dispatch, no debit. Proof: app/services/idempotency.py:215-216; tests/test_adversarial_five_claims.py:824-833.

**Fresh key, identical arguments, default LOG mode.** `MCP_UPSTREAM_DUPLICATE_GUARD` defaults to `log`, which detects and allows. The second call dispatches and receives a success receipt; the unit test asserts the outcome and `dispatch_count == 2`, not the debit. The failure lab, running the unmodified default, measures the same case as two dispatches and two debits. Proof: app/core/config.py:144-148; tests/test_upstream_retry_cap_enforcement.py:467-528; tests/test_failure_lab.py:188-191.

**Fresh key, identical arguments, ENFORCE mode.** Refused before dispatch with `duplicate_request_new_key`; `dispatch_count == 1`. On PostgreSQL, five concurrent identical new-key calls yield one dispatch, one debit, one success receipt and four denials. Detection runs by default in log mode; enforcement is opt-in. The guard covers upstream tools only (documented, not tested on local tools) and matches on permit id, tool and argument hash. It is bypassed by a permit's `allow_identical_repeats`, by OFF mode (measured on PostgreSQL only), and by the detection window expiring. The default window is 24 hours; the only expiry test uses a 60-second per-permit override, so the 24-hour window itself is not exercised. Proof: tests/test_upstream_retry_cap_enforcement.py:460-461, 534-597, 843-847; tests/test_duplicate_guard_postgres_concurrency.py:210-279, 286-468; app/core/config.py:144-151; docs/POLICY_ENFORCEMENT.md:212-227.

**Agent restart with a new key: nothing the single-run lab runs recovers it.** An agent that loses its key store and mints a new key for the same invoice pays twice in both configurations the single-run lab exercises for that case, native and gateway, under the default log-mode guard. Behind the gateway that is two dispatches, two debits and two distinct receipts, one per key. The test asserts the counts and that the keys differ; the lab document calls both correctly receipted, but no test asserts their offline validity. The lab never ran under enforce, so it shows neither that enforce would catch this case nor that it is uncatchable. In the scenario suite, T06 is documented as expected to fail for both gateway configurations: in configuration C the layer expected to catch the duplicate is the downstream keyed on the business operation id, not the gateway; in configuration D nothing catches it; and in both the gateway is expected to charge twice. CI enforces that expectation on every pull request; the repository holds no recorded figures for it. Proof: tests/test_failure_lab.py:183-199; docs/failure-lab.md:110-111, 128-133; docs/failure-lab-suite.md:233, 330-344; failure_lab/scenarios/t06_agent_restart_new_key.py:80-89; tests/test_failure_lab_scenarios.py:160-170.

**Against a correct native baseline, the gateway prevented no additional duplicate.** Where the downstream already honours a durable operation id, no additional duplicate effect prevented by the gateway was observed, and the lab fails if the gateway ever beats a correct baseline on effect count (tests/test_failure_lab.py:98-99). What the gateway measurably added, each against a different comparator: one payout instead of two, versus a naive downstream with no idempotency control (docs/failure-lab-suite.md:104-114, A versus D; docs/failure-lab.md:120-125); the original confirmation and receipt recovered by retrying the same key, versus an agent that lost a response the gateway had received (tests/test_failure_lab.py:110-125); and, versus the correct native baseline, no fewer effects, only a receipted `delivery_uncertain` state where the baseline left one attempt with no information (docs/failure-lab-suite.md:138-143). What it cost: the baseline resolved one attempt to a confirmed outcome and behind the gateway none was; one charge is retained against an unknown outcome, and the agent gets no resolution path inside the gateway (docs/failure-lab-suite.md:110, 144-146).

**Ambiguity after the send claim.** An upstream failure after the dispatch checkpoint is receipted `delivery_uncertain`, stays charged, and a same-key replay returns the identical receipt with no redispatch (tests/test_adversarial_five_claims.py:698-715; a fake executor raises the uncertainty in-process). After a real process death at that boundary, the same disposition exists only once the reconciler has terminalized the attempt; until then a same-key replay answers `idempotency_in_progress` (tests/test_mcp_postgres_multiprocess.py:1524-1583, an opt-in two-process SIGKILL suite on PostgreSQL that forces the reconciler). In production that wait is an 11,430-second idle threshold plus a five-minute sweep (docs/failure-semantics.md:322-325). A pre-dispatch failure refunds to net zero synchronously, with a `failed_refunded` receipt and no dispatch (tests/test_adversarial_five_claims.py:758-774; a fake executor raises a connection failure). A process death after the debit commits but before the checkpoint is refunded only when the reconciler adopts the orphaned debit, refunds it, releases the reservation and signs `failed_refunded` (tests/test_mcp_postgres_multiprocess.py:1652-1710); until that sweep runs, the debit and the reservation stand.

**Budget and caps.** No measured race overspent a permit's `max_credits` in gateway credits: 12 racing HTTP invokes with distinct keys against a 6-credit, cost-2 permit on SQLite stay within containment bounds (tests/test_adversarial_five_claims.py::test_claim2_concurrent_race_never_exceeds_cap); 12 concurrent reservations against a 6-credit cap admit 3 (tests/test_permits.py::test_concurrent_reservations_never_exceed_cap); the PostgreSQL row-lock path is covered by tests/test_permit_postgres_concurrency.py. tests/test_permit_budget_integrity.py covers sequential stale-read paths, not a race. The invariant campaign originally found a roughly 3x overspend on SQLite from a silently dropped `FOR UPDATE`; after a guarded UPDATE, a 7-credit cap under 10 racers yields 3 successes on both engines (docs/invariant-attack-report.md:171-268). A cap-of-one permit refuses the second key with `permit_max_calls_exceeded` and holds its slot after an uncertain delivery; caps count calls, they do not deduplicate (tests/test_upstream_retry_cap_enforcement.py:253-255, 312-313).

**Receipt tampering.** All ten edits inside `signing_input` broke the signature. Four envelope fields (`issuer`, `canonicalization`, `schema_version`, `keys_url`) are unsigned and were edited with the signature still valid. The gateway's own `POST /v1/receipts/verify` takes a `receipt_id` and re-checks the copy the gateway stores, so, handed the whole tampered bundle, it answered `valid: true` about a different object. That is the endpoint working as designed. The artifact in your hand must be checked offline over its `signing_input` bytes with an independent verifier: `failure_lab/verifier.py`, or the in-repo `b2a_sdk/src/b2a_sdk/receipt_verifier.py` (the SDK is built in CI but not published to PyPI; README.md:266; b2a_sdk/README.md:8-9). The product's bundle-oriented public MCP `verify_receipt` tool was off in the lab deployment and was not compared. Proof: failure_lab/scenarios/t10_receipt_tampering.py; docs/failure-lab-suite.md:299-328.

**Live deployment.** The repository's own unauthenticated harness (`gauntlet.py`, no credentials, run from an external network position; no third party is named) sent 57 single probes plus a 200-request, 50-agent swarm at the live surface and reported zero diagnostic findings. This is not an authentication verdict. It also recorded missing `content-security-policy` and `cache-control` headers, no 429 at 40 sequential requests, an 81 ms timing gap at its own 80 ms threshold, and, under the swarm with the rate limiter engaged, three 503s and one request that errored after 20.6 s. It tested the unauthenticated layer only and made no governed call; the committed output is a 2026-09-14 regeneration, and the original 2026-09-11/12 output was not retained. The production dispatch history, as last audited on 2026-09-01 (31 succeeded, 1 returned_error, 0 calls since the last restart), was operator-issued traffic from the repository's own scripts (`scripts/publish_live_proof.py`, `scripts/constant_test_loop.py`) against the operator's own `partner.echo` echo server in the same Railway project. "Partner" there is a runbook role label, not a company; the history contained no external caller, and no later document re-checks it. This is self-issued live gateway proof, not customer traction. Proof: docs/research/external-adversarial-2026-09-11/README.md and RESULTS.txt; DESIGN_PARTNER_GUIDE.md:62-63; docs/partner-first-tool-runbook.md:101-104. The 2026-09-01 audit document was removed from the public tree in commit b069225 and survives only in repository history (docs/reality-check-2026-09-01.md at b069225^, lines 52-59, 163-168 and 252-254).

## What remains a hypothesis

*Evidence class: hypothesis. Believed from code reading or documented expectation, not measured.*

- A fresh key with different arguments defeats every guard mode. The guard matches on a hash of tool name, arguments, wallet and permit (app/routers/mcp.py:1064-1073; app/services/mcp_dispatch_attempts.py:671-672), so any argument change is a new request. No test exercises it.
- Because the match is per permit, a retry under a different permit is never a duplicate, and the standard `POST /mcp` surface mints a separate auto-permit per client key, so by code reading a fresh-key retry there never matches in any mode (app/routers/mcp_standard.py:257-270; app/services/mcp_dispatch_attempts.py:628-648). No test exercises it.
- Enforce mode might refuse the lab's exact agent-restart rows, because both labs reuse the permit and resend byte-identical arguments under a new key inside the window, and unit tests refuse identical-argument new keys there (tests/test_upstream_retry_cap_enforcement.py:460-461). Neither lab ever ran under enforce; enforce applies only to the configured upstream tool and not to permits with `allow_identical_repeats` (app/services/mcp_dispatch_attempts.py:633-636; docs/POLICY_ENFORCEMENT.md:214-227); and a restart that re-requests a permit or changes any argument (a timestamp, a request id, or the native `idempotency_key` field the native-plus-gateway rows carry in the arguments) produces a new request hash that no mode detects.
- A real upstream completed its side effect. Tests count calls into fake executors and a simulated rail; a `success` receipt records that a valid response reached the gateway, not that money moved.
- The gateway is "a boundary the agent cannot route around". No test or control makes the upstream tool unreachable except through the gateway.
- Scenarios T11 (database restart), T13 (retention expiry) and T14 (standard MCP client) pass. They are documented expectations enforced by CI; the repository holds no recorded figures for them.
- The 2026-08-12 404s on the published proof and `/.well-known/jwks.json` are fixed in the tree: a `/.well-known/jwks.json` route exists (app/routers/well_known.py:735-747) and site/proof/receipt.json and trust-keys.json are pinned by tests/test_published_proof.py. The 2026-08-23 external-surface review verified the proof receipt's signature from the committed snapshot, not a live fetch, and found the deployment 103 commits behind `main`. Whether the live deployment serves either URL has not been probed since 2026-08-12 (docs/hard-run-report-2026-08-12.md; docs/external-surface-review-2026-08-23.md:17-21, 63-89).

## The architectural risks

These are architectural: properties of how the system is built, not deployment footnotes.

**Bypass paths.** The gateway is the only path to its one configured upstream tool only once the operator closes the tool's other routes. The repository positions the gateway as a boundary the agent cannot route around (WEDGE.md:62) but does not enforce one: the failure lab's baseline configurations call the tool directly (docs/failure-lab-suite.md:87-88), and no test or control makes the tool unreachable except through the gateway.

**Key custody.** The Ed25519 signing key is loaded from an environment variable into process memory. No HSM, no KMS, rotation by redeploy. If it is compromised, every receipt and permit becomes forgeable from that moment (docs/key-management.md:7-11, 45-53). Key distribution is first-party: keys are fetched over TLS from the same origin being audited, so a compromised issuing origin can serve a key set that validates forged receipts. Out-of-band pinning is not implemented, and the lab's independent verifier reports `ISSUER_TRUST_ESTABLISHED` as not established whenever the verifying key came from that origin, which is the product's current state (SECURITY_LIMITATIONS.md:40-47; docs/failure-lab-suite.md:191-198; failure_lab/verifier.py:338-350).

**Fresh-key semantics.** Identity is the caller's key. Two keys are two operations by design (docs/failure-lab-suite.md:330-344; docs/failure-semantics.md:231-232). The cross-key guard covers only identical-argument requests on the configured upstream tool and by default only observes them (app/core/config.py:144-150), and because the guard hashes the arguments, any argument change under a fresh key is a new request in every mode, an inference from the hashing design that no test exercises. The mitigation is in the agent: derive and persist the idempotency key from the business operation, not the attempt; nothing at the boundary recovers a key the agent lost (docs/failure-lab.md:128-133).

**Downstream ambiguity.** `delivery_uncertain` is a truthful statement that the outcome is unknowable to the gateway, not a resolution. Closing it is manual and outside the boundary.

**Receipt completeness.** There is no transparency log; receipts prove what happened, never what did not. Pre-permit denials leave no receipt. A local tool that crashes after its side effect leaves a debit with no receipt, in permanent manual review. Revoking a compromised key makes every receipt it signed unverifiable (docs/agent-accountability.md:132-165).

**Durable state.** Audit chains are tamper-evident, not immutable; a database administrator can delete rows. The replay guarantee lasts exactly as long as the idempotency record row. No retention policy or automatic purge exists today (the record's `expires_at` column is defined at app/db/models.py:1075 but unused), so nothing shortens it. The day an operator adds a purge job, TTL index or table trim, a same-key retry after the purge is a new operation: the gateway dispatches again and charges again, and whether a second downstream effect lands depends on the downstream's own idempotency, not on the gateway. This is the documented expectation of failure_lab/scenarios/t13_retention_expiration.py, whose record-removal case is descriptive and has no recorded run in the repository.

**Upstream reconciliation.** A dispatch claim is treated as stale only after a fixed 11,430-second idle threshold and the next five-minute sweep, so nominal pickup is about 11,730 seconds plus backlog. A crash before the claim is reconciled to an automatic refund; a stale claim after it is receipted `delivery_uncertain`, stays charged, and is never redispatched or refunded automatically (docs/failure-semantics.md:139-143, 169-173, 306-309, 321-325). Revocation landing after the `prepare` transaction does not stop a call already holding its reservation (docs/failure-lab-suite.md:243-262).

## What you still have to build

1. **Close the other routes.** Egress allowlist or proxy so the tool is reachable only through the gateway. The repository also leaves the gateway's own egress allowlist for the single partner origin to production operators (SECURITY_LIMITATIONS.md:96-98).
2. **Decide the guard mode.** Default `log` only observes. `MCP_UPSTREAM_DUPLICATE_GUARD=enforce` refuses identical-argument fresh-key retries on the configured upstream tool, under the same permit, inside the window (24 hours by default), with PostgreSQL for the concurrent case. It does not cover changed arguments, and by code reading it does not cover the standard `POST /mcp` surface.
3. **Key the agent on the business operation.** Persist the idempotency key with the invoice or order, not the attempt. Nothing at the boundary recovers a key the agent lost.
4. **Make the downstream idempotent too.** The remote-side guarantee holds only if the upstream honours the forwarded key. If you can change your downstream, do that first.
5. **Build the reconciliation loop.** Every `delivery_uncertain` receipt needs a person or job that queries the upstream with the forwarded key and closes it.
6. **Protect and pin the signing key.** Hardware custody is not shipped. Pin the public key set out of band in your own verification so it does not trust the origin it audits; the product does not implement pinning.
7. **Run production posture.** `TRUST_MODE_ENABLED=true`, legacy unpermitted MCP off, proof surfaces off, durable PostgreSQL with migrations. Boot refuses the permissive combination; it does not audit a running deployment.
8. **Treat receipts as gateway evidence.** Verify the signed fields offline. `issuer` and `keys_url` are unauthenticated; a receipt is not proof the payment completed.

## Closing

Intent is not authority. The agent's wish to refund, pay or deploy is a request; the permit is the authority; the receipt is the record of what the gateway decided and observed. AMW holds that line on one tested path, one adapter in front of one configured upstream tool, under conditions the operator must keep true. It does not make a retry safe when the agent forgets its own key, does not know what the downstream did, and cannot prove an action it did not record. The proof is self-issued, not customer traction. Judge it on those terms.
