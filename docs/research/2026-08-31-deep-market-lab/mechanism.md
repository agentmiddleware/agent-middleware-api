# Conditional mechanism theorem and adversarial boundary

Research date: 2026-08-31. Scope: the supported remote MCP one-tool path. This is a static review plus a finite explanatory model; it is not an application test run, a security certification, or customer validation. No application, test, schema, public API, billing policy, or deployment files were changed.

The defensible claim is **one durable caller identity can acquire at most one gateway send right and one governed debit, with uncertainty retained and gateway evidence linked**. It is not semantic duplicate detection, guaranteed completion, proof of upstream business truth, or a bound on the business value of an arbitrary mutation.

## Exact conditional statement

Let the action identity be `K = (wallet_id, normalized governed-MCP endpoint, caller idempotency_key)`. Let `H` be the canonical hash of `{tool_name, arguments, wallet_id, permit_id}`. JSON-RPC correlation IDs and REST framing are intentionally absent. The concrete identity and payload construction are in [mcp.py:595](/Users/sellers/Documents/GitHub/agent-middleware-api/app/routers/mcp.py:595); database namespace normalization is in [models.py:985](/Users/sellers/Documents/GitHub/agent-middleware-api/app/db/models.py:985).

Assume all of the following:

1. The deployed implementation and migrations preserve the inspected unique constraints, transaction boundaries, and claim fencing. All participating workers see one continuous authoritative database history; no restore, split brain, privileged rewrite, or unreviewed alternate writer invalidates that history.
2. The caller authenticates to the correct wallet and supplies the same valid `K` and `H` for every retry of that logical action. A semantic action is not independently recognized by the gateway. Collision resistance and authentic signing keys are cryptographic premises, not established by this review.
3. The action uses the supported governed MCP execution path and the inspected single-send adapter. The gateway, operators, adapter, and configuration are trusted to execute that path. Upstream `initialize` and transport setup do not themselves perform the consequential mutation being protected.
4. Signed permit authority is valid when the atomic authorization/reservation step admits the action; configured credit and call limits are the intended limits. All ledger changes affecting this theorem use the governed accounting rules.

Then, for this identity and continuous database history:

- At most one dispatch attempt and at most one operation-keyed debit can be retained. A successful one-shot claim requires the debit link, and only the activation owning that claim may enter the adapter's tool send. There may be **zero** sends after a claim.
- A later activation cannot acquire the existing claim. Reuse of `K` with a different `H` conflicts; exact replay cannot generate another debit or gateway dispatch through this path.
- Once terminal, the dispatch disposition cannot transition to a different terminal disposition. `delivery_uncertain` and `response_rejected` retain their charge under the current policy. A refundable outcome permits one correlated accounting reversal, not a claim that a remote effect was rolled back.
- The signed receipt binds the gateway's recorded identity, hashes, permit, accounting amount, outcome, and evidence references. Its signature authenticates those bytes under the trusted key; it does not independently authenticate upstream business facts.

An eventual signed terminal response additionally requires database/signing availability, eventual scheduling of reconciliation, sufficient storage, and valid retained state. This liveness condition is separate from the safety claim. The local callable path has a documented post-side-effect crash/operator-review exception and is not covered by the remote recovery theorem.

## Why uncertainty trades progress for safety

Consider two histories after a durable debit and claim:

```text
H0: claim commits -> worker dies before send                         -> no effect
H1: claim commits -> send -> upstream commits -> response lost -> die -> effect
```

Both can leave the restarted gateway with the same durable claim and no result. Without an additional authoritative upstream observation, a recovery decision based only on that gateway state cannot distinguish them. Resending can restore progress in H0 but repeat the effect in H1 when the upstream lacks reliable deduplication. Refusing to resend preserves the gateway's one-send property but allows H0 to remain charged with no effect.

This is an indistinguishability argument for this observation boundary, not a claim that an upstream lookup could never resolve uncertainty. Retaining the charge is an accounting/incentive policy; the one-send safety property itself does not logically require charging an uncertain call. A late upstream effect may still arrive after an uncertainty receipt, so that receipt is not proof the external system is quiescent.

An authoritative lookup returning `not_applied` **at one instant** is not necessarily permission to replace the action. A hypothesized schedule is: the original worker is paused after receiving its claim right; the gateway records uncertainty; the provider lookup is empty; an operator creates a replacement under a new key; then the old worker resumes and its one allowed request takes effect. Each identity can satisfy the theorem while the semantic action occurs twice. Even if the original worker is gone, a queued/in-flight provider operation can create the same issue. A reconciliation record should authorize replacement only with evidence of **final absence**: the original operation is canceled/fenced and cannot later apply, or the provider's authoritative lifecycle is terminal, or both attempts share a durable idempotent business identifier. Otherwise record “not observed as of this lookup; replacement safety unknown.” This is an operational boundary and a model-permitted schedule, not a newly reproduced application exploit.

Forwarding an idempotency key is also insufficient for exactly-once completion. Even an upstream with perfect deduplication cannot complete an action it never receives. Exactly one business effect additionally needs eventual successful delivery and an upstream implementation that atomically couples durable deduplication with that business effect, for the correct namespace and retention period. The repo's “only if the upstream honors the key” language must be read as a necessary condition, not sufficient proof.

## Three decision-changing risks and falsifiers

1. **Semantic identity and path coverage are a customer integration obligation.** Fresh keys, omitted keys, changed wallet/namespace, direct upstream access, or a force-dispatch operational workaround bypass semantic deduplication. Changing only the permit under the same `K` changes `H` and conflicts; it does not create a new identity. Payload binding rejects drift on an existing key; it does not decide that a new key repeats the same intended transaction. **Falsifier of useful partner fit:** the partner's real retry/orchestrator path cannot retain one identity or route the consequential mutation exclusively through the gateway. **Smallest decision test:** have the partner show its actual retry storage and credentials/path, then run one effect-then-response-loss case with an exact replay. Do not add a semantic intent engine without named customer evidence.

2. **Response/accounting truth can be mistaken for business-effect truth.** `isError: true` becomes a refundable `returned_error` ([upstream_mcp.py:1023](/Users/sellers/Documents/GitHub/agent-middleware-api/app/services/upstream_mcp.py:1023), [mcp.py:1949](/Users/sellers/Documents/GitHub/agent-middleware-api/app/routers/mcp.py:1949)). A provider may commit a mutation and then report error; the gateway can honestly refund its fee while the business effect exists. A valid success response may also represent an accepted/asynchronous operation rather than a committed effect. A point-in-time empty lookup does not rule out a later effect and must not automatically authorize a fresh-key replacement. **Falsifier of the proposed contract:** the partner cannot supply an authoritative lookup, distinguish effect-then-error from effect-free rejection, or establish finality when a replacement is proposed. **Smallest decision test:** specify the partner's error/effect/finality semantics and manually reconcile one known partial/error result; use a call ceiling for the pilot. Do not market credit caps as mutation-value guarantees.

3. **Continuity and evidence trust remain operator assumptions.** Restore gateway state to before K while retaining the remote effect: K can appear new. Compromise the signing key: authentic-looking statements can be forged. Compromise the serving origin/key distribution: verification can trust an attacker-selected key. These do not require defeating the one-shot transition. [SECURITY_LIMITATIONS.md:33](/Users/sellers/Documents/GitHub/agent-middleware-api/SECURITY_LIMITATIONS.md:33) explicitly disclaims KMS, external transparency, immutable storage and public RTO/RPO guarantees. **Falsifier of deployment fit:** the buyer requires independent nonrepudiation, lossless failover, immutable auditing, or regulated production data now. **Smallest decision test:** confirm acceptance of the dedicated, synthetic/redacted pilot boundary and the no-automatic-retry restore procedure. Freeze broader compliance/HA claims; no speculative infrastructure expansion is warranted by this review.

## Documentation contradictions requiring correction

These are static, actionable contract findings, not newly executed application failures. They were reported to the parent immediately; this research task did not edit product docs.

- [failure-semantics.md:142](/Users/sellers/Documents/GitHub/agent-middleware-api/docs/failure-semantics.md:142) says revoked permits cannot retrieve replay evidence. `validate_replay_access` intentionally ignores expiry/revocation and checks stable wallet/key/signature binding ([permits.py:405](/Users/sellers/Documents/GitHub/agent-middleware-api/app/services/permits.py:405)). Correct wording: revocation prevents new admissions, while authenticated evidence replay remains available subject to binding/auth checks. Another key is denied when the permit is key-bound; not all wallet permits necessarily bind a key.
- [failure-semantics.md:55](/Users/sellers/Documents/GitHub/agent-middleware-api/docs/failure-semantics.md:55) says a local raised exception means the tool did not run. The callable is entered before the exception/refund branch ([mcp.py:1527](/Users/sellers/Documents/GitHub/agent-middleware-api/app/routers/mcp.py:1527)); it can commit a side effect before raising. That row cannot infer absence of effect.
- [failure-semantics.md:57](/Users/sellers/Documents/GitHub/agent-middleware-api/docs/failure-semantics.md:57) calls a rejected response “confirmed executed.” The adapter establishes that a response could not safely be retained, not that the intended business mutation committed. Replace with “response observed/unusable; business effect requires upstream confirmation.” Likewise a signed `success` is the gateway's accepted response classification, not a general remote commit proof.

## Executed model, provenance, confidence

The self-contained [mechanism_model.py](/Users/sellers/Documents/GitHub/agent-middleware-api/docs/research/2026-08-31-deep-market-lab/mechanism_model.py) enumerated **340 reachable states and 1,526 transitions** for one durable identity, two competing activations and zero to two upstream effects per send. All seven groups of assertions passed: at most one send/debit/refund; debit before send; no uncertainty refund; absorbing terminal; fixed claim owner; unchanged exact replay; unchanged rejected payload drift. The [results JSON](/Users/sellers/Documents/GitHub/agent-middleware-api/docs/research/2026-08-31-deep-market-lab/mechanism-model-results.json) contains shortest counterexample traces and hashes of the inspected files at model execution time.

Independent model review found that an earlier version incorrectly permitted lost-ack recovery after the attempt had become uncertain. The implementation requires that recovery still observe `dispatch_claimed`, so the model now permits recovery only while `phase == "claimed"`. Re-execution removed eight invalid transitions (1,534 → 1,526), with the same 340 reachable states and passing assertions. The separate scenario where a worker already received its send right and pauses before network dispatch remains in the model. The critique corrected the abstraction; it was not an application defect.

The model deliberately permits an upstream to commit zero, one or two effects and to commit after the gateway classifies an outcome. It exhibits charged uncertainty with identical gateway observations but different effect truth, refunded errors with an effect, and one send with two business effects. Fresh-key/restore repetition and empty-lookup/replacement/late-old-send duplication are explicitly labeled composition arguments, not enumerated failover or application tests.

The model **assumes**, rather than verifies, atomic SQL, unique identity, authorized admission, debit fencing and cryptographic trust. It proves only the bounded transition-system properties written into this abstraction. It does not establish implementation refinement, all concurrency schedules, numerical correctness, auth isolation, PostgreSQL failover, liveness, or partner behavior. It is useful because it makes the claim limits and counterexamples explicit, not because its passing assertions certify the application.

Existing technical-program execution is separate. The accepted program record now binds 572 files to ten exit-zero commands: 1,688 full-suite passes, 65 default-pool PostgreSQL regressions including 20 calls and 20 stable replays, 36 actual singleton-application-pool cases, nine crash cases, 18 concurrency cases, five datetime/trust cases, six posture cases, the release gate, Ruff, and mypy. Counts overlap and must not be summed. The earlier rapid-invoke and baseline failures remain historical evidence; the bounded caller-session fix passed final independent review without pool inflation or weaker assertions. These application results were not executed by this mechanism review. They accept local gates G0–G5 for the frozen source, not production or the partner-owned G6 experiment. No partner-run result has been established here.

Confidence: high in the conditional reasoning and cited static contract discrepancies; strong bounded local implementation evidence under the accepted source/configuration; not verified for customer workflow compliance, business-effect semantics, production continuity, compromised-key scenarios or deployments outside the documented pilot.
